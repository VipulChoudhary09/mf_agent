An open ended equity scheme predominantly investing in large cap stocks. PPFAS

India's Top Companies. Broad, cost efficient exposure to India's top 100

## What Is This Fund Trying to Do?

The fund seeks to provide low-cost, broad large-cap exposure with implementation
designed to reduce trading and impact costs while keeping returns close to the
index over time.

- **Broad Diversification:** Exposure to India's top 100 large-cap companies
  provides broad market coverage.
- **Lower Costs:** Reduced expenses compared mean more of your returns stay in
  your pocket.
- **Low Active Share:** Lower risk in choosing stocks and managing the portfolio
  versus the benchmark index
- **Smart Execution:** Investing strategies that reduce execution costs and
  improve net returns.

## Who decides what to Buy?

**Rules driven Stock Selection**

- Stock Selection: Nifty 100: Covers top 100 companies as per Market cap
- Stock Weightage: Based on stock's free float market capitalization. Weight of
  any holding capped at 10% **Team Chooses Optimal Implementation for Smarter
  Execution**
- Positions are implemented using efficient instruments and timing (e.g.,
  futures, gradual rebalancing) to achieve portfolio alignment with top 100
  companies based on market capitalization.

## Understanding Smart Execution Strategies

1. **Single-Stock Futures:** Use Stock Futures to get better execution price.
   Use Futures to BUY the stock when at discount to Cash market. When a stock's
   near-month futures trade below the cash price, the fund may use futures to
   create exposure more efficiently, subject to limits and regulations. Example
   (As of 25th November 2025, expiry date for Nov Futures): BANKBARODA (Nov
   Futures VWAP 284.3, Cash VWAP 285.9, Futures Discount -0.56%), BHARTIARTL
   (-0.40%), INDUSINDBK (-0.38%), ADANIENT (-0.32%), HINDALCO (-0.30%), SBIN
   (-0.29%), LICI (-0.27%), EICHERMOT (-0.25%), CANBK (-0.23%), TECHM (-0.23%).
   Source: NSE Data. The above data is representative and for illustrative
   purposes only. Actual prices, discounts, and execution may vary, and there is
   no guarantee of any future outcome.
2. **Index Futures:** Using Index Futures when attractive. During market
   dislocations, Index Futures may be at a discount to the Index. Similarly, if
   index futures trade below index levels, the fund may use them to obtain
   exposure efficiently and reduce implementation costs. Example (Dates in 2020:
   29-05-2020, 16-03-2020, 19-03-2020 etc., with discounts ranging from -0.45%
   to -0.90%). Source: NSE Data.
3. **Merger Arbitrage:** Merger related opportunities. When a company in an
   Index is merging with another firm, Buy the stock which is at a discount to
   the merger ratio. Example: HDFC Ltd and HDFC Bank Merger. Shareholders of
   HDFC Ltd to get shares of HDFC Bank. 25 shares of HDFC Ltd get 42 shares of
   HDFC Bank Ltd. On 13th April 2022, HDFC Ltd was at a 3.3% discount to HDFC
   Bank (25 shares of HDFC Ltd = INR 59,462, 42 shares of HDFC Bank Ltd = INR
   61,528). Buy HDFC Ltd instead of HDFC Bank.
4. **Smarter Rebalancing of the Fund:** Index rebalances are announced in
   Advance. But Index funds transact only on the rebalancing date, which can
   result in significant inflows or outflows for stocks being added or removed.
   When the Nifty 100 changes, the fund may rebalance gradually rather than on
   the exact index date to seek better execution prices. Example: NSE Indices
   Press Release, changes effective Sept 30, 2025.
5. **Using low Active Share Opportunistically:** Better execution of
   Demerger/Special Situations related selling. Around corporate actions like
   demergers or special situations, the fund may phase entries and exits to
   reduce impact costs while keeping overall active share low (under 10%). When
   a company undergoes a demerger, quite often one of the two resulting firms
   gets removed from the Index. Removal from Index can create a selling pressure
   on the firm. Index Funds are forced to exactly replicate the Index and remove
   the demerged firm as soon as it is removed from the Index. Example: ITC Ltd
   and ITC Hotels Demerger.

## This fund is ideal for investors who:

- Want broad exposure to India's top 100 companies by Market Capitalization
- Prefer lower costs compared to typical active funds
- Value a strategy that aims to deliver index-like returns
- Have a long-term investment horizon (5+ years)
- Understand that equity investments can be volatile
- Appreciate tactical efficiency in implementation

## This fund may NOT be suitable for investors who:

- Seek to significantly outperform the index
- Want concentrated bets on specific stocks or sectors
- Prefer active stock selection based on fundamentals
- Have a short-term investment horizon
- Cannot tolerate equity market volatility
- Expect the fund to avoid overvalued stocks
